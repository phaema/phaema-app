# Estate strategy

PHAEMA helps a council keep its estate in line with its asset management strategy, property by property. It is
organised around the RICS guidance on strategic public sector property asset management. Everything here is part of
the core of PHAEMA: there is nothing to switch on.

It works at two levels: the **strategy** (its objectives, action plan and progress reports to the board) and each
**property** (why the council holds it, what it plans to do with it, and whether it is fit for the job).

## The strategy, its objectives and the action plan

**Reports**, **Estate strategy** shows the council's adopted strategy: its title, period, who approved it and the
minute reference, a link to the published document, its policy principles and its **objectives**.

Administrators record the strategy in **Manage data**, **Estate strategies**: the title, the period, the approval
and the policy principles, then the objectives. Only one strategy is adopted at a time; adopting a new one marks
the old one replaced, and it stays in the history.

Each **objective** has a number, a title, an owner and a target date. As the RICS guidance suggests, it can also
record (all optional) the corporate aim it serves, the expected outcomes, how success is measured, the financial
effect, and the risks and opportunities.

Each objective has an **action plan**. On the objective's page, its owner or an administrator selects **Add to the
action plan** and either gives a project or case reference (for example PRJ-2026-001 or CAS-2026-0058), whose
progress then shows by itself, or describes a stand-alone action with an owner and a target date. The owner of an
action can mark it in progress, done or dropped. Actions due show on their owner's My work.

### Status and progress

PHAEMA **suggests** each objective's status from its action plan:

| Suggested | When |
|---|---|
| Off track | An action is past its target date, a linked project has missed a milestone, a linked case is past target, or the objective's own target date has passed. |
| At risk | An action is due within a month, or a linked project is slipping, paused, over budget or carrying a high risk. |
| On track | Everything else that is still open. |
| Achieved | Every action is done. |

The objective's owner then **reports** the status with a short progress note (**Report progress**), so the board
sees a judgement with the evidence beside it. Where the two differ, the page and the board report say so. An owner
who has not reported for 90 days is prompted on My work.

### The board report

**Board report** gives the progress on the strategy as a page, to print or download as Word: a summary; every
objective with its status, details, progress note and actions; the **exceptions** (anything late, over budget or
carrying a high risk); Asset Challenges decided in the last year and in progress; and the asset strategies and
suitability across the estate. A confidential project appears only to those who can see it.

## Each property

The three questions for every property are **why do we hold it** (its holding purpose), **what are we going to do
with it** (its asset strategy) and **is it fit for the job** (its suitability).

## Holding purposes and sub-categories

Every property has one of five **holding purposes**: Operational, Investment, Community, Surplus or Held for
disposal. These five are fixed, because asset valuation, the Government Property Data Standard export and some
reports rely on them.

Under each, the council keeps its own **sub-categories**, for example Operational: Offices, Depots, Libraries.
Administrators change the list in **Manage data**, **Holding purpose sub-categories**. Choose one for a property in
**Edit this property**, under Classification. A sub-category must belong to the property's holding purpose.

## Asset strategy

A property's **asset strategy** is the decision about its future. The council's list starts with:

| Asset strategy | Means | Afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| Retain as is | Keep it for its present purpose with routine maintenance. | |
| Retain and invest | Keep it and invest to improve condition, suitability or energy use. | Start a project |
| Remodel or share | Change the layout, or bring in other services or partners. | Start a project |
| Release space | Give up part: a floor, a wing or surplus land. | |
| Dispose later | No long-term need; dispose when the timing is right. | |
| Dispose now | Surplus: start the disposal. | Start a disposal project |
| Community asset transfer | Transfer to a community organisation to run. | Start a disposal project |

Administrators change the list in **Manage data**, **Asset strategy options**, including what PHAEMA offers
afterwards.

Each decision keeps **who decided** (for example Head of Estates, Cabinet or the Asset Management Board), the
**meeting date**, the **minute reference** and the **reason**. Earlier decisions stay as the property's history.

**Parts follow the whole.** A strategy can be recorded at any level. A unit or building with none of its own follows
the estate or site it is part of, shown as "follows DM-N01". Give a part its own strategy where it differs, for
example one block on an estate earmarked for disposal.

There are two ways to record one, both from the **Strategy** section of the property's page:

- **Start an Asset Challenge** to review the property's future afresh (below).
- **Record an asset strategy already decided**, for a decision made earlier or elsewhere. Give the reason and any
  minute reference. It is marked "recorded directly". Strategies can also be loaded from a spreadsheet with the
  property import.

### Strategy review

Recording a strategy sets the property's **strategy review date**: the council's usual period later (five years to
start with, set in **Module settings**, Estate strategy), unless another date is given. As the date falls due, the
managing surveyor sees **Strategy review** on My work, and the property's page shows Due soon or Overdue.

## The Asset Challenge

The **Asset Challenge** is the review process used by many councils to test whether each property is still needed
and how it could serve the council better. It is a case type with six tasks:

1. **Strategic purpose:** does the property serve a council aim, and will it in future?
2. **Opportunities and risks:** partners, planning, the market, condition, compliance.
3. **Performance appraisal:** cost, use, condition, suitability and income.
4. **Option appraisal:** retain, invest, remodel or share, release, dispose.
5. **Consultation:** services, ward members, partners and occupiers.
6. **Outcome:** the asset strategy decided.

Record the **proposed asset strategy** on the case once the options have been weighed, then ask for approval with
your recommendation. By default the supervisor approves in PHAEMA before the decision; administrators can change
this for the case type.

Where a board, committee or cabinet member decides, record the decision with **Decided by**, the **meeting date** and
the **minute reference**. When the decision is Approved or Approved with conditions, the strategy decided is put on
the property with the reasons, and the next review date is set. A refused or withdrawn challenge leaves the
property's strategy as it was.

If the strategy is one that starts a project, the property's page offers **Start a project** or **Start a disposal
project**, with the property already in it.

## Suitability

A **suitability grade** says how well a building suits the service in it today, whatever its condition:

| Grade | Means |
|---|---|
| A | Good: suits the service well. |
| B | Satisfactory: minor shortcomings. |
| C | Poor: shortcomings that hamper the service. |
| D | Unsuitable: cannot serve its purpose without major change. |

Select **Record suitability** on a building's page and give the grade, the date and what it rests on (layout,
access, location, size, image). Each assessment is kept, so the trend can be seen.

## The estate at a glance

**The estate at a glance** (linked from Reports and the Estate strategy page) shows: strategy reviews overdue and due, Asset Challenges in
progress, how many properties are on each asset strategy, the holding purposes and sub-categories, and the
suitability grades. Every figure links to the list of those properties. The property list can also be filtered by
sub-category, asset strategy, suitability and strategy review.

The report builder has columns for the sub-category, asset strategy, when it was decided, which property it follows,
the strategy review date and the suitability grade.

## Who can do what

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| Administrators | Everything: the strategy and its objectives, the sub-category and asset strategy lists, and correcting decisions in Manage data. |
| An objective's owner | Add to and remove from its action plan, and report its status and progress. |
| An action's owner | Mark the action in progress, done or dropped. |
| Surveyors | For the properties they manage: start an Asset Challenge, record an asset strategy already decided, and record suitability. |
| Supervisors | Approve an Asset Challenge's recommendation, as for other cases. |
| Others | Look. |
