# Projects

A project is a larger piece of work than a [case](cases.md). It can cover several properties (a development or a
programme), properties the council is selling (a disposal), or no property yet: an acquisition starts with a
**proposed site**. A project has a lead, a team, milestones, tasks, notes and documents, and moves through five
phases separated by four **gateways**.

| Kind | Typical example |
|---|---|
| Development | Redeveloping an estate into new units |
| Acquisition | Buying land for a new depot |
| Disposal | Selling surplus land in lots |
| Programme | Energy efficiency upgrades across many buildings |
| Strategic review | An options appraisal for a service's buildings |

## Starting a project

Administrators and surveyors: select **Projects**, then **Start a project**. Give it a name and type, choose the
lead and add the sponsor and dates. For an acquisition, describe the **proposed site** and, if known, its title
number and the vendor. PHAEMA adds starter tasks for the type of project.

## Phases and gateways

| Phase | Ends with |
|---|---|
| Idea | Gateway 1: go ahead with a business case (usually an officer) |
| Business case | Gateway 2: approval to proceed (usually Cabinet or a capital board) |
| Delivery | Gateway 3: completion confirmed |
| Completion | Gateway 4: close-out review |
| Close | The project is closed |

Record each decision under **Gateways**: who decided, the outcome, the date, the decision reference (for example the
Cabinet minute) and, if you like, the report. **Approved** moves the project to the next phase; **Deferred** keeps
it where it is; **Rejected** stops it. A project can also be paused, restarted or stopped, with a reason.

## Milestones and status

Add milestones with a target date, then keep the forecast and achieved dates up to date. The project shows
**Milestone missed** (red) when a target passes without being achieved, and amber when a forecast is later than its
target, a task is overdue or the project is paused.

## The team and who can do what

Add colleagues to the **Team** with their role on the project, and outside parties such as agents or architects.

- Everyone with a role can see projects, except confidential ones.
- The lead, team members who are surveyors, and administrators can change the project.
- Any team member, including a legal contributor, can add notes and documents.

**Confidential projects** can only be seen by their team and administrators; others cannot find them in lists, on
property pages or in reports. Acquisitions start confidential. A confidential project's documents can only be opened
from the project page.

## On other pages

A property's page lists the projects it is part of, including those of the building or estate it belongs to. My
work shows **My projects**, and project tasks and milestones you own appear under what needs attention.
